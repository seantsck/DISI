## Findings worth keeping

- **Affiliate names do not name organizations.** The 021 affiliation rule had the New Orleans Zephyrs as Houston's from 1999 to 2016. The official
  team records (team 588) say Milwaukee 1993-1996, Houston 1997-2004, Washington 2005-2006, the Mets 2007-2008, the Marlins 2009-2016. Three stints
  (Antonio Osuna 2005, Pedro Astacio 2006, Chan Ho Park 2007) are corrected and the rule now carries one entry per parent.
- **The official record resolves almost everything once it is asked per season.** Of 1,706 non-foreign team stints, 1,383 recorded organizations
  match the record, 319 unresolved ones resolve (84 of them to Dodgers affiliates), 3 are corrected, 1 has no MLB parent (Bakersfield Blaze 1995,
  a co-operative club the record files under the Office of the Commissioner). The Mexican League clubs are filed the same way and stay
  foreign-professional, never an organization.
- **Resolving organizations makes more seasons multi-organization, honestly.** 24 players had a recorded two-organization season before; 32 do
  after. 14 seasons (12 players) are bracketed by a canonical trade and game dates; 38 have no canonical transaction (career moves between other
  clubs, which DISI's Dodgers-scoped transaction record does not hold), 4 are before dated game logs, 1 has three organizations.
- **2020 is evidenced, not assumed.** The official 2020 regular-season schedule holds 0 games at Triple-A, Double-A, High-A, Single-A, short-season
  and Rookie, against 875 (short-season A) to 3,192 per level in 2019 and 1,900 to 2,707 at the levels that existed in 2021; the 2020
  major-league schedule holds 973. 17 season-gap players have 2020 missing; for 16 it is the only gap. Andy Pages also misses 2019, which was
  played, and stays open.
- **The Pioneer League's classification is a season fact.** Rookie (16) for every season on record (1988-2018), Rookie Advanced (5442) in 2019 and
  2020, Independent (23) from 2021. The Rookie Advanced designation is real but the official record does not give it to any season DISI holds.
- **Exact dates reveal within-season sequences.** Elián Herrera's first High-A game (2007-04-05) came before his first Low-A game (2007-04-20);
  Pedro Báez (2014), Ramón Troncoso (2008) and Roki Sasaki (2025) appeared in Triple-A after their MLB debut in the same season. Each is a pending
  review; whether it was a cameo, an option or a rehabilitation assignment is a person's decision.
- **The game-log window is not a disagreement.** For nine veterans the first appearance in the 2006+ window is not the debut; the person-record
  debut stands and no source conflict is recorded.
- **Highest level moves only where the organization moved.** Carlos Santana's 2006 Inland Empire stint is a Dodgers affiliate season, so his highest
  level at a Dodgers affiliate is High-A (2006), not Low-A (2007); Jorbit Vivas's 2023 Oklahoma City stint makes his Triple-A. No whole-career
  highest level changes, and the audited no-MLB cohort's distributions (051) are unchanged.
